3️⃣ Export Atlas + Material Maps
⚠️ Height (parallax) maps are expensive in Tomb Engine, and they change more than performance. A height map also switches SSAO off for that material and disables bullet holes, explosion marks and other decals on it. Parallax is also incompatible with animated, double-sided and mirror textures. Prefer applying height to individual textures rather than a whole atlas, so you can judge each one and drop it where it isn’t worth the cost.
⚠️ Some of your tiles have transparency. Their holes are kept flat in the height map, but parallax shifts the texture before the holes are cut, so a cutout’s edges move with the relief and can be eaten away or show through. Check fences, grates and foliage in game, and drop Height for those textures if it looks wrong.
📐 Every map atlas must share the same dimensions. Texturetool guarantees this automatically when you export all maps together here. But if you export maps in separate passes, e.g. just the normal map now, other maps elsewhere or later, make sure the tile size and layout match every time, or Tomb Engine will throw an error and not load your material maps!

✨ Create Variations, Tile
Creates jittered copies (hue / brightness / saturation / contrast / grain / rotation / wrap shift) appended to the atlas, handy for breaking up obvious tiling repetition. Same seed reproduces the same set.
🪞 Origami Frame, Tile
Folds the texture into a concentric frame, a ridge or stripe texture becomes nested rings, as if the strip were folded around all four edges. The fold is symmetric and seams continuously through the corners.
🏗️ Build Pattern, Tile
Arranges this texture into a built surface, brick, coursed / cobbled stone, tile, herringbone, planks, a plank floor, shingles or pipes, by laying it into cells split by recessed joints, each cell a jittered sample so it doesn't read as repeats. The result is a fresh, seamlessly tiling tile; the dark joints become real depth in the material maps.
🏚️ Age & deformation
Breaks up the machine-perfect grid for ruins and old stonework. Everything here is seeded and stays seamless, a flush city wall is all zeroes.
🧱 Behind the cells
What shows through the joints, and through anything Missing pieces takes out. Pick another atlas tile here to lay blocks over a wall, instead of over a blur.
🌾 Surface noise , off
Grain, pitting and grain-direction laid over the pattern itself. Your texture probably has its own already, this is for flat or over-cleaned sources.
🌾 Surface Noise, Tile
Lays procedural grain into the texture itself, seamlessly. Material maps are generated from the diffuse, so this is what gives a flat or over-cleaned texture something for them to read.
🪟 Stained Glass, Tile
Splits the tile into glass cells separated by lead came. Cell colours come from the texture itself or a palette. The result carries a glass + metal multi-material and an emissive map, so the window glows in the dark.
